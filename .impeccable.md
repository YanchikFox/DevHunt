# DevHunt — Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
Mixed developer audience — students and bootcamp grads through mid/senior engineers and indie hackers. They come to find collaborators, join projects with momentum, and build things that matter. Usage spans all times of day and contexts: morning coffee browsing on mobile, late-night kanban sessions on desktop, lunch-break profile updates. The platform must feel equally welcoming to a CS sophomore discovering open source and a senior engineer evaluating a side-project co-founder.

### Brand Personality
**Warm · Collaborative · Welcoming**

The emotional goal: the feeling of getting a thoughtful code review from a senior you respect — precise and helpful, never condescending. Like a well-organized workshop where everyone has a role and the work is real. Not a hackathon (too chaotic), not a job board (too transactional), not a social network (too casual). A platform where serious builders feel at home and newcomers feel capable.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Crafted warmth with editorial clarity.**

Both light and dark modes are required — each must feel intentional, not just an inversion.

- **Light mode**: Warm off-white surfaces (not pure white), tinted toward amber/sand. Strong typographic hierarchy. Feels like a well-designed technical publication on a Sunday morning.
- **Dark mode**: Deep warm-gray surfaces (not cold navy, not pure black). Feels like a focused dev session in a well-lit room, not a hacker cave.
- **Accent color**: Warm amber-to-copper range — energetic without being aggressive, distinct from the blue/purple monoculture. A secondary cool-blue allowed for interactive states only.
- **Typography**: Pair a display font with quiet humanist character (something that reads as precise and slightly warm — not geometric-cold, not friendly-rounded) with a body font optimized for reading longer project descriptions and docs.

**Anti-references (hard NOs):**
- Floating blobs, blue-to-purple gradients, glowing cards — current DevHunt hero, to be replaced
- Cold sterile enterprise software (Jira, Confluence, ServiceNow)
- Cutesy pastel illustrations and bouncy animations

**References for feel (not copy):**
- The precision of a well-designed developer tool (Raycast, Linear) but warmer
- The community warmth of a thoughtful open source project homepage
- Editorial clarity of a technical magazine: hierarchy, whitespace, purposeful density

### Design Principles
1. **Human-scaled precision**: Every component should feel like it was made by a thoughtful person, not generated. Attention to spacing rhythm, consistent optical alignment, no accidental asymmetry.
2. **Warmth through material**: Tinted neutrals (amber hue at low chroma), rounded corners that feel considered not bubbly (6–10px radius), texture through typography weight variation not decoration.
3. **Depth through hierarchy, not decoration**: Type size, weight, and spacing carry the visual hierarchy. Color accents are rare and purposeful — not used to decorate every card.
4. **Purposeful duality**: Light and dark modes each have their own character. Light = open, airy, daytime energy. Dark = focused, contained, late-night energy. Neither is an afterthought.
5. **Developer-respectful density**: Show information at useful density. Don't pad every element to feel "spacious" — respect that developers can read. Use generous whitespace at the *section* level, tighter spacing at the *component* level.
